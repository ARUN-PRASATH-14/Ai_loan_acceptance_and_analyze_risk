CHAPTER 1: SECTOR-SPECIFIC MASTER DIRECTIONS OVERHAUL (NOVEMBER 2025 - 2026)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Background & Restructuring:
On November 28, 2025, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) decommissioned the monolithic 2016 KYC Master Direction and introduced ten sector-specific Master Directions. These directions govern Scheduled Commercial Banks (SCBs), Regional Rural Banks (RRBs), Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs), Payments Banks, and Urban Co-operative Banks (UCBs).

1.2 Core Regulatory Mandates:
- Customer Due Diligence (CDD): Standardized baseline for natural persons, legal entities, trusts, and partnerships.
- Digital Onboarding: Integration of Video-based Customer Identification Process (V-CIP) and e-KYC channels.
- Central KYC Registry (CKYCR): Direct uploading of 14-digit KYC Identifier (KIN) records within 14 days of account activation.
- AML/CFT Obligations: Mandatory reporting of Cash Transaction Reports (CTR) and Suspicious Transaction Reports (STR) to FIU-IND.

CHAPTER 2: OFFICIALLY VALID DOCUMENTS (OVD) & CUSTOMER IDENTIFICATION (CIP)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Officially Valid Documents (OVDs):
1. Proof of Identity (PoI): Passport, Driving Licence, Voter's Identity Card, Proof of possession of Aadhaar number (Masked Aadhaar format), Job card issued by NREGA duly signed by an officer of the State Government, Letter issued by the National Population Register.
2. Tax Identification: Permanent Account Number (PAN) Card mandatory under Income Tax Rule 114B. Form 60 accepted strictly for small agricultural landholders and individuals exempt from income tax.
3. Utility Bills recency: Municipal tax receipt, electricity bill, piped gas bill, or landline phone bill not older than 60 days.

2.2 Aadhaar Privacy & Storage Rules:
- Regulated Entities (REs) must redact or mask the first 8 digits of the Aadhaar number when storing physical or digital records.
- Biometric e-KYC: Conducted via UIDAI registered biometric scanners with explicit customer consent.
- OTP-based e-KYC: Non-face-to-face OTP accounts carry a maximum balance cap of ₹1,000,000 and total credit cap of ₹2,000,000 per financial year. Full physical or V-CIP KYC must be completed within 12 months.

CHAPTER 3: DIGITAL ONBOARDING & VIDEO CUSTOMER IDENTIFICATION PROCESS (V-CIP)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Operational Standards for V-CIP:
- Mandatory infrastructure: Secure domain connection operated by bank staff.
- Real-time interaction: Live audiovisual call, liveness detection, facial matching against Aadhaar/PAN photo, and geo-fencing (capturing live GPS location within Indian territorial borders).
- Instant PAN validation: Real-time verification against the Income Tax Department (ITD) database.

3.2 Business Correspondent (BC) Assisted KYC:
- Banks are authorized to deploy accredited Business Correspondents (BCs) for electronic document capture and biometric e-KYC validation in rural and unbanked regions.

CHAPTER 4: RISK CLASSIFICATION & PERIODIC KYC UPDATION (2025-2026 TIMELINES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Risk Categorization Matrix:
- Low Risk: Salaried employees with verifiable salary accounts, government employees, pensioners, small agricultural landholders, retail borrowers with pristine repayment history.
- Medium Risk: High Net Worth Individuals (HNIs), sole proprietorships, traders, self-employed professionals, and non-resident Indian (NRI) clients.
- High Risk: Politically Exposed Persons (PEPs), trusts, NGOs, charities, companies with multi-layered offshore ownership structures, clients operating from high-risk geographic jurisdictions.

4.2 Periodic KYC Updation Frequency & 2025-2026 Relief Extension:
- High Risk Accounts: Full KYC updation at least once every 2 years.
- Medium Risk Accounts: Full KYC updation at least once every 8 years.
- Low Risk Accounts: Full KYC updation at least once every 10 years.
- RBI Relief Directive (2025-2026): For Low-Risk individual accounts where periodic updation fell due in calendar year 2025, the completion deadline is extended to June 30, 2026 (or 1 year from original due date, whichever is later). During this window, accounts shall remain operational without debit freeze, subject to transaction monitoring.

CHAPTER 5: ULTIMATE BENEFICIAL OWNER (UBO) & ANTI-MONEY LAUNDERING (AML)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) Thresholds:
- Corporate Entities: Controlling ownership interest threshold is 10% of shares, capital, or profits. Any natural person exercising control through voting rights or executive management must undergo full KYC.
- Partnership Firms / Unincorporated Associations: Natural person holding > 10% of capital or profits.
- Trusts: Settlor, trustees, and beneficiaries holding ≥ 10% interest.

5.2 FIU-IND Reporting Timelines:
- Cash Transaction Reports (CTR): Submitted by 15th of the succeeding month for cash transactions > ₹1,000,000.
- Suspicious Transaction Reports (STR): Mandatory submission within 7 working days of arriving at suspicion.

CHAPTER 6: DIGITAL LENDING CODE, KEY FACT STATEMENT (KFS) & APR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 Digital Lending Guidelines:
- Mandatory Key Fact Statement (KFS): Every retail and MSME loan agreement must include a transparent KFS detailing:
  a) All-inclusive Annual Percentage Rate (APR) comprising nominal interest rate, processing fee, documentation charges, legal valuation fee, and stamp duty.
  b) Total repayment schedule and EMI table.
  c) Cooling-off / Look-up period: Minimum 3 days for personal/unsecured loans, 7 days for housing loans. Borrowers can exit the loan without penalty by repaying principal + pro-rata APR.

6.2 Loan Servicing & Limits:
- Loan disbursal must occur directly into the borrower's bank account without routing through third-party Loan Service Provider (LSP) pool accounts.
- Automatic credit limit increases without explicit consent are prohibited.

CHAPTER 7: PRIORITY SECTOR LENDING (PSL) & AGRICULTURAL NORMS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 Priority Sector Lending Targets:
- Scheduled Commercial Banks PSL Target: 40% of Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC).
- Agriculture Target: 18% of ANBC (10% dedicated to Small and Marginal Farmers - SMFs).

7.2 Collateral-Free Agricultural Credit:
- Per RBI directives, Scheduled Commercial Banks are mandated to dispense with collateral requirement for agricultural loans up to ₹160,000 (extended up to ₹200,000 for banks utilizing digitized land record portals like Tamil Nadu e-Revenue Chitta/Adangal).

7.3 Interest Subvention Scheme (ISS) & KCC:
- Short-term crop loans up to ₹300,000 disbursed via Kisan Credit Card (KCC) carry a benchmark interest rate of 7.00% p.a.
- Government 2.00% Interest Subvention (IS) + 3.00% Prompt Repayment Incentive (PRI) lowers effective interest rate to 4.00% p.a. for disciplined farmers.

CHAPTER 8: PRADHAN MANTRI MUDRA YOJANA (PMMY) & MSME CREDIT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.1 MUDRA Loan Categories (Micro & Small Enterprises):
- Shishu: Loans up to ₹50,000 (zero collateral, zero processing fee).
- Kishore: Loans above ₹50,000 up to ₹500,000.
- Tarun: Loans above ₹500,000 up to ₹1,000,000.
- Tarun Plus: Loans above ₹1,000,000 up to ₹2,000,000 (Introduced under Union Budget 2024-2025 for entrepreneurs who have successfully repaid previous Tarun loans).

8.2 Credit Guarantee Fund Trust for Micro and Small Enterprises (CGTMSE):
- Collateral-free credit facility up to ₹50,000,000 for eligible MSME units with guarantee coverage ranging from 75% to 85%.

CHAPTER 9: INTEGRATED OMBUDSMAN SCHEME & GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.1 Internal Grievance Escalation:
- Banks must resolve customer complaints within 30 calendar days.
- Every bank must appoint a Principal Nodal Officer (PNO) and Internal Ombudsman (IO).

9.2 Escalation to RBI Integrated Ombudsman:
- If unresolved within 30 days or if customer is unsatisfied with bank response, complaints can be lodged on the RBI CMS Portal (https://cms.rbi.org.in) or via Toll-Free Number 14448.

CHAPTER 10: CUSTOMER FAIR PRACTICE CODE & RECOVERY GUIDELINES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10.1 Recovery Agent Code of Conduct:
- Contact hours restricted strictly to 08:00 AM to 07:00 PM.
- No intimidation, harassment, physical coercion, or breach of privacy.
- Recovery calls to third parties or family members not designated as guarantors are strictly prohibited.
